PAGCOR suspended the licenses of three online gaming operators on Tuesday, citing repeated failures to comply with anti-money-laundering (AML) reporting obligations, according to a statement from the regulator. The move brings the Philippine regulator's 2026 enforcement tally to 11 suspensions or revocations, the highest year-to-date figure in five years of published records.

The three operators — PlayInnovation Gaming Inc., LuckyStar Gaming Corp., and Asia8 Entertainment Holdings — were each licensed under PAGCOR's Philippine Inland Gaming Operator (PIGO) framework, the domestic online regime that replaced the offshore POGO sector dismantled in 2024. All three will have their platforms dark for at least 90 days pending a compliance audit, the statement said.

## What the regulator cited

PAGCOR's notice points to three categories of breach, each traceable to specific filings the operators were required to submit to the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC):

- Failure to file Covered Transaction Reports (CTRs) on player deposits exceeding PHP 500,000 ($8,700) within the five-day window required under Republic Act 9160, as amended.
- Inadequate customer due diligence (CDD) on VIP accounts flagged by AMLC in a Q4 2025 referral list.
- Late or missing Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) on patterns AMLC had specifically highlighted in its Q1 2026 typologies bulletin.

"The licensees were given written notice in January and a 60-day remediation window," PAGCOR chairman Alejandro Tengco said in the statement. "The audit findings in April showed the gaps were not closed." Tengco added that two of the three operators had been on an internal watchlist since 2025.

None of the three operators responded to requests for comment before publication. LuckyStar's investor relations page was offline at the time of writing.

## The 2026 enforcement pattern

The three suspensions are not isolated. PAGCOR has taken formal action against 11 licensees so far in 2026, compared with seven in the same period of 2025 and four in 2024, according to the regulator's public enforcement log. Six of the 2026 actions cite AML-specific failures; the remainder involve responsible-gaming or advertising breaches.

The pattern aligns with two external pressures. First, the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) removed the Philippines from its grey list in February 2025 conditional on sustained supervisory output from AMLC and sectoral regulators, of which PAGCOR is the largest by licensee count. Second, Tengco's term runs through 2027, and his public remarks since 2024 have repeatedly tied PIGO license retention to AML cooperation.

Analysts at AGB Nippon, who track Asia-Pacific gaming enforcement, said the April action was consistent with guidance PAGCOR issued to licensees in late 2025. "The regulator telegraphed this," said AGB analyst Ben Blaschke. "The surprise is which three names, not that three names got picked."

## Stakeholder impact

For licensed PIGO operators, the practical read is narrow. Compliance teams should expect AMLC's Q1 2026 typologies bulletin to be treated as an enforceable checklist, not guidance. Operators with VIP books drawing from jurisdictions on AMLC's high-risk list — notably, several Southeast Asian source markets — are the most exposed.

For investors in listed Philippine gaming names, the direct revenue impact is limited. The three suspended operators are mid-tier by gross gaming revenue (GGR, the amount wagered minus winnings paid out) and collectively accounted for an estimated 4% of PIGO sector GGR in 2025. The larger question is whether DigiPlus, Bloomberry Resorts, and other scaled players face the same audit methodology, which PAGCOR has said will be applied sector-wide by Q3.

For AMLC, the action is a visible win at a moment when the agency is preparing its first post-grey-list mutual evaluation input.

## Timeline

- **January 2026** — Written notice and 60-day remediation window issued to the three licensees.
- **March 2026** — AMLC Q1 typologies bulletin published; PAGCOR audit teams deployed.
- **April 22, 2026** — Suspensions announced; 90-day minimum dark period begins.
- **July 2026 (earliest)** — Earliest possible reinstatement, subject to compliance audit.
- **Q3 2026** — Sector-wide audit methodology rollout, per PAGCOR's stated plan.

## The Takeaway

PAGCOR is not merely enforcing. It is publishing a methodology. The January notices, the March audits, and the April suspensions follow the sequence AMLC laid out in its post-grey-list commitments, and the regulator's enforcement cadence suggests more actions in Q2 and Q3. Operators treating the 2025 guidance as advisory are reading the room incorrectly. The name to watch next is whichever Tier 1 licensee first draws an audit team — that event, not this one, will tell the market how deep the enforcement goes.
