# Indonesian Police Bust 75 Illegal iGaming Sites, Name Philippines as Operator Refuge

Indonesian National Police arrested more than 320 foreign nationals and shut down 75 illegal iGaming operations in West Jakarta, according to Indonesian law enforcement disclosures. Authorities explicitly named the Philippines as a likely destination for operators displaced by Cambodia's earlier expulsion of unlicensed iGaming businesses — putting PAGCOR on notice that a regional enforcement wave is moving in its direction.

## The Displacement Pattern

The arrests follow a traceable chain. Cambodia's crackdown on Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs, offshore-licensed online gambling businesses that targeted foreign players) and unlicensed iGaming pushed operators into adjacent markets. Indonesia's West Jakarta bust is the most concrete evidence yet that displaced operators have been reconstituting elsewhere in the region rather than exiting the market.

Indonesian authorities also disclosed that approximately 200,000 children have been exposed to online gambling — a figure that adds political weight to the enforcement action and signals that regional governments are under domestic pressure to act, not merely coordinate.

## The Philippines Implication

Indonesian police naming the Philippines as a relocation destination is not an accusation directed at PAGCOR, but it is a warning. The Philippines is currently navigating its own post-POGO regulatory transition, and the status of iGaming licensing under PAGCOR's revised framework remains in flux [verify]. An influx of displaced, previously unlicensed operators — some potentially carrying POGO-era infrastructure and player databases — would arrive at a moment when the regulator is already restructuring oversight.

PAGCOR has not issued a public response to the Indonesia bust at the time of writing [verify].

## World Cup Timing

Indonesian authorities separately warned against illegal betting tied to the FIFA World Cup 2026, which kicks off June 11. High-profile sporting events are established catalysts for unlicensed operator activity across Southeast Asia. The convergence of displaced operators seeking new bases and a major betting event on the immediate horizon sharpens the near-term risk window for Philippine regulators.
