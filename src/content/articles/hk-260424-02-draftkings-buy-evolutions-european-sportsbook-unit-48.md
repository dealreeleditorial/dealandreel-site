DraftKings Inc. (NASDAQ: DKNG) has agreed to acquire Evolution AB's European sportsbook operations for $4.8 billion in cash and stock, the companies said in parallel disclosures, in what would be DraftKings' largest-ever transaction and its first meaningful entry into Europe's regulated sports-betting markets. The deal is expected to close in Q3 2026, subject to approvals from gaming regulators across multiple European jurisdictions.

The price tag is roughly three times the $1.56 billion DraftKings paid for [Golden Nugget Online Gaming in 2021](/tag/draftkings), until now its largest deal. For Evolution (STO: EVO), a company that has publicly positioned itself as a pure-play B2B live-casino and random-number-generator (RNG, software-driven slots and table games) supplier, the transaction is a clean exit from an operator-side unit that never fit the core thesis.

## What the filings say

Under the agreed terms, DraftKings will acquire the unit — described in the disclosures as Evolution's European sportsbook operator business, inherited and built out following the 2020 NetEnt acquisition — with a closing targeted for Q3 2026 [verify]. The cash-and-stock mix and the identity of financial advisors to either side were not detailed in the [announcement reviewed here](https://investors.draftkings.com/press-releases).

Jason Robins, chief executive of DraftKings, said the acquisition would give the company "licensed access to European regulated markets at scale" and cited expected cost savings from platform consolidation and cross-sell of US iGaming content. Martin Carlesund, chief executive of Evolution, said proceeds would be redeployed into live-casino capacity and capital returns to shareholders, consistent with guidance the company issued when it opened a [strategic review of the unit in late 2025](/tag/evolution).

## The price

At $4.8 billion, the transaction implies roughly 8–10 times forward EBITDA if the unit runs at mid-hundreds of millions of euros in revenue — a multiple closer to what Evolution itself trades on as a supplier than to recent European sportsbook-operator comparables. Entain's BetMGM joint venture, Penn Entertainment's ESPN Bet arrangement, and the Flutter-FanDuel structure all offer reference points, but none map cleanly: DraftKings is paying a control premium for a licensed footprint, not a minority stake or a brand licence.

That matters because organic European entry is effectively closed to a new US entrant. Every major market — the United Kingdom, Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands, Italy, Spain — is licensed, saturated, and dominated by Entain, Flutter, bet365, Betsson and the [former Kindred assets now inside La Française des Jeux](/tag/m-and-a). M&A is the only entry ticket, and DraftKings has chosen to pay supplier-level multiples for it.

## Financing and balance-sheet strain

DraftKings reported roughly $1.2 billion in cash on its Q4 2025 balance sheet [verify], well short of what a cash-heavy structure would require. The stock component of the consideration offers some relief, but the company is likely to need an incremental debt raise or equity issuance to fund the cash portion — a move that will draw analyst focus once the mix is disclosed. DraftKings shares traded [verify] on the announcement; Evolution shares moved on the Stockholm session reflecting both the divestiture proceeds and the removal of a non-core overhang.

## Regulatory path

The realistic risk to a Q3 2026 close is regulatory, not antitrust. Change-of-control reviews will be required at minimum from the [UK Gambling Commission](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/), the [Malta Gaming Authority](https://www.mga.org.mt/), the Swedish Spelinspektionen, and — depending on the unit's German footprint — the Gemeinsame Glücksspielbehörde der Länder (GGL). Each runs to its own timetable. A six-month clearance window across four regulators is ambitious; precedent from Entain, Flutter and [888's recent European change-of-control filings](/tag/regulation) has typically stretched nine to twelve months when the acquirer is new to the jurisdiction.

US Committee on Foreign Investment (CFIUS) review does not apply — the seller is European, the asset is European — but DraftKings will face its own scrutiny from US state regulators that license its sportsbook, each of which reviews material changes to corporate structure.

## What to watch

Three disclosures will determine whether the market's initial read holds. First, the cash-and-stock split and any associated financing package — the equity dilution risk is the most immediate share-price variable for DraftKings. Second, the unit's standalone revenue and EBITDA, which neither side has released in segment detail and which will allow analysts to test the 8–10x multiple implied by the headline. Third, the regulatory clearance calendar: any slippage beyond Q3 2026 will compress the cost-savings case DraftKings has sold to its own shareholders. The deal's logic — that entry into European sports betting now requires paying supplier multiples for operator assets — will stand or fall on those three numbers.
