# BETBY Goes Exclusive on QTech's SEA Rail 50 Days From World Cup Kickoff

BETBY has signed an exclusive sportsbook supply agreement with QTech Games, removing independent sportsbook competition from one of Southeast Asia's largest aggregator platforms roughly 50 days before the FIFA World Cup 2026 cycle begins driving handle (total amount wagered). The deal bundles BETBY's core sportsbook and its esports-native feed, Betby.Games, into QTech's single-API stack for the aggregator's SEA-facing operator clients, according to BETBY's announcement.

For operators integrated with QTech, the practical effect is a narrowed shortlist at the worst point in the four-year sports cycle: those who want BETBY's product through QTech can take it; those who want an alternative through the same rail no longer have one.

## What's in the deal

The agreement covers BETBY's full sportsbook product — pre-match, in-play, and the Betby.Games esports feed — distributed exclusively through QTech to operators on the aggregator's platform. QTech, headquartered in Stockholm but built around Asia distribution, runs a one-integration model competing with SoftSwiss, Pragmatic and Salsa Technology.

Several scope questions remain unanswered in the public materials. It is not clear whether exclusivity applies across QTech's full distribution footprint or is limited to SEA-facing clients [verify], nor whether operators currently live on competing sportsbooks via QTech — historically including BetConstruct and Altenar — will be grandfathered, sunset, or migrated [verify]. Commercial terms, including revenue-share bands and any minimum guarantees, were not disclosed.

## Why operators should care

The single-API model compounds switching cost. An operator already certified on QTech who wants a sportsbook other than BETBY now needs a separate direct integration and a second tech stack to run alongside the aggregator rail. That is the mechanism by which distribution turns into lock-in, and it lands as PH-licensed books, Curacao-licensed operators targeting Vietnam, Indonesia and Thailand, and AGCC licensees running Asia desks finalize their World Cup trading setups.

The esports inclusion is the quieter half of the deal but arguably the more defensible. In several SEA-facing grey markets, esports represents a meaningful share of handle, and the pool of credible esports trading suppliers is materially smaller than the mainstream sportsbook field. Bundling Betby.Games into the same exclusive narrows that field further on the QTech shelf.

## What to watch

The structural read is that aggregator-level exclusives, not direct operator contracts, are emerging as the binding constraint in SEA B2B sportsbook distribution. Watch how Altenar, BetConstruct and smaller sportsbook B2Bs that share aggregator shelves respond — whether by seeking reciprocal exclusives elsewhere, pushing harder on direct operator deals, or accepting a smaller addressable market on QTech-adjacent rails. Clarification on the regional scope of the exclusivity and the fate of incumbent integrations will determine how sharply the shortlist actually narrows before kickoff.
