# PAGCOR's New Live-Dealer Framework Puts SEA Supply Chain on Notice

**The Philippine regulator has released rules that reshape compliance obligations for studios streaming live-casino content across Southeast Asia — and the transition window may be shorter than operators expect.**

PAGCOR, the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation, has published a new regulatory framework governing live-dealer studios that stream to players outside the Philippines, according to a May 4, 2026, report by CDC Gaming. The Philippines hosts the dominant production corridor for live-casino content in Asia — Evolution Gaming's Cebu facility, megaFUNalo, and Pragmatic Play-affiliated studios all hold Philippine-based licenses — making the framework a de facto supply-chain event for any B2B operator sourcing live content for Southeast Asian products. Specific rule text has not yet been independently confirmed against PAGCOR's official filings; details below are attributed accordingly.

## What the Framework Covers

The new rules apply to studios that hold PAGCOR live-dealer licenses and direct their output to players in markets outside the Philippines — the configuration that describes virtually every major content supplier operating from the country. That scope alone signals a departure from prior PAGCOR frameworks, which focused primarily on domestic operations and the now-defunct Philippine Offshore Gaming Operator (POGO) category.

Whether the framework distinguishes between studios operating on a business-to-consumer basis and those supplying content on a B2B basis to downstream operators remains unconfirmed [verify against PAGCOR.gov.ph]. The distinction matters: a B2B supplier's compliance obligations would flow through to every operator client sourcing its feed. If PAGCOR has drawn that line, the practical burden lands further down the distribution chain than many operators may currently assume.

Specific technical requirements — including streaming standards, data-localisation obligations, and any know-your-customer (KYC) pass-through rules — have not been confirmed from a second source and are not reported here as settled fact [verify]. The same applies to any new capital or minimum staffing thresholds for studio operators [verify].

## The Transition Timeline Question

The effective date and transition period for existing licensees have not been confirmed [verify against [PAGCOR.gov.ph](https://www.pagcor.ph) or a second B2B source]. This is the operationally critical variable. A short runway — anything under 90 days — would require studios to begin compliance work immediately, before many of their operator clients have even reviewed the framework. A longer window reduces urgency but does not eliminate the need for early assessment, particularly for studios whose technical infrastructure may require modification.

For operators sourcing live-dealer content from Philippine studios, the transition timeline is not an internal supplier problem. If a studio misses a compliance deadline and PAGCOR suspends or restricts its license, the downstream effect is feed interruption — tables go dark, products go offline, and operators face the choice of sourcing replacement content on short notice or carrying gaps in their live-casino lobbies. In a product category where player retention is tied closely to table availability and brand consistency, that is a material commercial risk.

## How the Major Studios Are Positioned

Evolution Gaming, which operates what is widely regarded as the largest live-casino production facility in Asia from its Cebu base, has not publicly commented on the new framework as of publication. The company's scale — and the breadth of its operator client base across Southeast Asia — means its compliance posture will be watched closely by the industry. Pragmatic Play, whose Philippine-affiliated live studio operations supply a large number of white-label and branded operator products across the region, is similarly positioned [verify studio structure]. megaFUNalo, a PAGCOR-licensed operator, also falls within scope.

None of the three had issued public statements addressing the framework at the time of writing. Absence of comment is not unusual at this stage of a regulatory cycle; studios typically conduct internal legal review before making public commitments on compliance timelines.

## What Operators Should Be Doing Now

For B2B operators whose live-casino products depend on Philippine-sourced content, the practical response is not to wait for supplier communications. The appropriate first step is to identify which content feeds in the live lobby carry PAGCOR-licensed origin, then request written confirmation from each studio supplier of their compliance status and expected timeline. That information should feed directly into product risk registers.

Operators with significant live-casino revenue concentration in a small number of Philippine studios face the highest exposure. Diversification of studio supply — across jurisdictions including Malta, Georgia, and Romania, where other major live-content hubs operate — is a structural hedge that compliance events of this type tend to accelerate.

PAGCOR's enforcement mechanism for non-compliance has not been confirmed [verify]. If the regulator attaches license suspension or restriction as a consequence — the standard tool in its enforcement toolkit — the risk to operators is not hypothetical.

## What to Watch

The two variables that will determine how disruptive this framework becomes are the confirmed effective date and the B2B-versus-B2C scope distinction. If PAGCOR publishes implementing rules that place pass-through compliance obligations on downstream operators — not just on the studios themselves — the compliance surface expands significantly. Watch for official publication on [PAGCOR.gov.ph](https://www.pagcor.ph) and for Evolution, Pragmatic Play, or megaFUNalo to issue supplier advisories to their operator clients; those communications, when they arrive, will be the clearest signal of how the industry is reading the transition window.
